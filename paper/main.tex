\begin{abstract}
Rule-based HVAC controllers remain widely deployed, and a supervisory layer that adapts their exposed
parameters without modifying them is a low-cost way to improve them, but it is unclear when such adaptation
helps. We study two sources of values for such a layer, each evaluated separately on matched 30-day episodes
of the Smart Buildings Challenge simulator with pre-specified tests: re-selection from a library of
weather-conditioned profiles, and bounded online estimation and feedback rules. Within the building families
the profiles were tuned on, a one-time gate that re-selects daily only after mild starts outperformed
persistence-based re-selection by 0.504 percentage points (95\% station-cluster interval [0.337, 0.704]; 98
episodes), but a registered replication on 40 new episodes was inconclusive, and the profiles did not transfer
to new architectures. On a blind test of unseen architectures, a learned optimal start and a duct
static-pressure reset, a few bounded rules with no offline training, reduced the loss of a tuned fixed
controller by 2.82\% over its 10 composite architectures (95\% architecture-cluster interval [1.48, 3.94]), improving 11 of all 12.
Unchanged, the layer reduced loss by 5.71\% on a later version of the controller and by 3.37\% on a trained
neural controller, where its pre-specified per-architecture criterion failed; an energy-targeted rule reduced
the loss of a model-based controller by 1.88\%. On the competition leaderboard the comfort rules gave no gain
or a loss. An exact decomposition bounds a rule's benefit by the share of the controller's loss in the term it
targets; post hoc, gains followed this share (Spearman $\rho = 0.80$), and five of seven predictions
registered from it held. In simulation, these results support targeting supervisory rules to what a controller
leaves uncorrected rather than applying them by default.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Heating, ventilation and air-conditioning (HVAC) systems must balance occupant comfort against energy cost and carbon
emissions as conditions change. Model predictive control and reinforcement learning exploit this variability but
may need models or long training and can be hard to deploy and transfer across buildings~\cite{drgona2020mpc,wang2020rl};
many installed systems still run rule-based logic.

A middle ground keeps the controller and lets a supervisory layer adapt its parameters during operation, using a small
library of weather-conditioned profiles tuned in advance or values estimated online from the building, with feedback, within fixed bounds.
With a library, timing matters: re-selecting often tracks the weather but may switch profiles for little benefit, and
since profile effects accumulate over days, they must be judged over long horizons.

We ask when profiles should be re-selected, when the layer should learn instead, and when it helps at all; we study
the two sources separately and propose no rule that chooses between them. Using a public
rule-based controller~\cite{malyshev2026k07} in the Smart Buildings simulator~\cite{goldfeder2024suite,goldfeder2026challenge},
we compare policies on matched 30-day episodes and test each source on sets frozen in advance: the profiles on their own
building families, and learning on new architectures and three further controllers. Our contributions are (i) a supervisory layer over an unmodified rule-based controller with two sources of values,
profiles, whose \emph{selection} we separate from reassignment \emph{timing}, and online adaptation of
building-specific quantities by bounded rules (\cref{sec:method}); (ii) a matched evaluation of reassignment
schedules over three weather years, in which frequent reassignment paid mainly after mild starts, and a one-time gate
built on this pattern modestly beat persistence in a pre-specified test (\cref{sec:reselect}); (iii) out-of-family
and blind tests: the frozen profiles did not transfer, while online estimation with feedback did, in pooled gain, on unseen architectures, unchanged on a
later version of the controller and on a neural one, and retargeted to energy on a model-based one (\cref{sec:learn}); and (iv) an account of when such a layer helps: its gain is the benefit in
the term it targets, bounded by that term's share of the controller's loss, minus its cost in the others, which, found post hoc,
fits its gains on four controllers; on shared scenes, five of the seven
predictions registered for it held (\cref{sec:when}).

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Building control.}
Benchmarks such as BOPTEST~\cite{blum2021boptest}, Sinergym~\cite{jimenez2021sinergym}, CityLearn~\cite{vazquez2019citylearn}
and the Smart Buildings Control Suite~\cite{goldfeder2024suite} make controllers comparable across buildings and weather,
the Smart Buildings Challenge~\cite{goldfeder2026challenge} scores them on hidden scenarios, and
Building2Building~\cite{taboga2026b2b} targets transfer across buildings. Besides model predictive control
(MPC)~\cite{drgona2020mpc} and reinforcement learning~\cite{wang2020rl}, residual policy learning keeps an existing
controller and learns a correction to its actions~\cite{silver2018residual,johannink2019residual}; we keep the controller
without learning a policy, adapting its exposed parameters or bounding one command, and ask how much this can add, given
what the controller leaves uncorrected (\cref{sec:when}).

\paragraph{Adapting rule-based controllers.}
Control has long been adapted to conditions, from gain scheduling~\cite{rugh2000gain} to supply-temperature
reset~\cite{raftery2018reset} and optimal start, which learns from past recoveries when to
start~\cite{seem2016return,kim2024optimalstart}. Contextual Bayesian optimisation tunes parameters to the context under
constraints~\cite{fiducioso2019safe,xu2024primaldual}; our rules are bounded, with no online black-box optimisation.
Distribution tuning of rule-based HVAC constants~\cite{pacheco2026distribution} argues for comparing adaptive schemes
with tuned static ones. Timing is a design choice too: event-triggered MPC re-optimises only when
needed~\cite{yang2023event}, and we compare schedules that re-select profiles from a fixed library on matched episodes.
The layer's main rules are a recovery-based optimal start and the duct static-pressure trim-and-respond of
Guideline~36~\cite{ashrae2021g36}; we contribute evidence on when they help, not new mechanisms. Because choosing
configurations or rules after seeing outcomes is optimistic~\cite{cawley2010overfitting}, we separate development from
frozen evaluation, as in pre-registration~\cite{nosek2018prereg}, and test where a lack of transfer would stay
hidden.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
%======================================================================
\subsection{Problem formulation}\label{sec:problem}
\paragraph{Controller and layer.}
A stateful rule-based controller maps observations $x_t$ and parameters $\theta_t$ to actions $u_t = C(x_t, \theta_t)$; $B$
denotes the fixed baseline configuration. A supervisory layer sets $\theta_t$ during operation without
modifying $C$, from one of two sources (\cref{fig:framework}): a profile library $\Phi$ whose entries a schedule $\pi$
selects at daily decision points (\cref{sec:profilesource}), or a state $\xi_t$ updated from the building's
response, with $\theta_t = g(\xi_t) \in \Theta$, where $\Theta$ contains only quantities the controller already exposes, within
bounds fixed in advance; this source may also floor the static-pressure command of each air handler $a$, $u_{t,a} \leftarrow \max(u_{t,a}, f_{t,a})$
once requests have raised $f_{t,a}$ above its minimum (\cref{sec:learning}). For profiles the weather regime proposes a profile and the schedule decides whether to adopt it;
for learning a rule fixed in advance determines whether to act and the building's response how much. At runtime neither
source uses future weather or the reward, and the learning state is reset to fixed values every episode.

\paragraph{Objective.}
The simulator reward is $r_t = -(0.6\,q_t + 0.2\,c_t + 0.2\,e_t)$, with non-negative normalised comfort regret $q_t$, energy
cost $c_t$ and carbon $e_t$. The loss $\ell(s,\pi)$ of a policy $\pi$ (a configuration, schedule or layer) in episode $s$ is the mean of $-r_t$ over its $|T(s)| = 8{,}640$
scored steps, and the gain of $\pi$ over a comparator $\pi'$ on a matched set $S$ with weights $w_s$ is the reduction in
pooled loss
\begin{equation}
  G_S(\pi;\pi') = 100\left(1 - \frac{\sum_{s\in S} w_s\,\ell(s,\pi)}{\sum_{s\in S} w_s\,\ell(s,\pi')}\right).
  \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
It is not a mean of episode-wise gains. Policies are compared by their difference in gain over $B$,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_S(\pi_1,\pi_2) = G_S(\pi_1;B) - G_S(\pi_2;B)
  = 100\,\frac{\sum_{s\in S} w_s\,[\ell(s,\pi_2) - \ell(s,\pi_1)]}{\sum_{s\in S} w_s\,\ell(s,B)},
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
in percentage points (pp) of baseline-referenced gain. The profile source is evaluated against $B$, its
policies compared by \cref{eq:delta}; the learning source is evaluated against G30, the tuned configuration it runs on
(\cref{sec:setup}), by \cref{eq:gain} with equal weights.

\subsection{Profile source}\label{sec:profilesource}
\paragraph{Regimes and decisions.}
The frozen library $\Phi = \{\theta_E, \theta_C, \theta_M, \theta_H\}$ holds one profile per temperature regime. On day
$d$ of a 30-day episode a decision point occurs at step $t_d = 228 + 288(d-1)$, and the mean observed outdoor temperature
$\bar T_d$ over the preceding 228 five-minute steps (19\,h) is mapped to a regime $k_d = r(\bar T_d)$: extreme-cold ($E$) if $\bar T_d < -15\,^\circ$C, cold ($C$) if
$-15 \le \bar T_d < 11\,^\circ$C, mild ($M$) if $11 \le \bar T_d < 24.5\,^\circ$C, and hot ($H$) otherwise. At each $t_d$ the schedule $\pi$ decides whether to replace the
active profile by $\theta_{k_d}$. All schedules start under $B$, and profiles are applied in place, keeping the
controller's integrators and the plant state (\cref{alg:supervisor} in \cref{app:profiles}).

\paragraph{Schedules.}
All adopt the first decision's profile and differ only in later changes: \textbf{PS5} makes none (select once), \textbf{PD5} may
reassign daily, \textbf{LIST} and \textbf{WEEK} only on predefined days (sparse), \textbf{TRIG2} only
after a new class appears in two successive complete windows (persistence), and \textbf{MG}, the one-time gate, follows PD5
if the first class is mild and PS5 otherwise, a choice it never revisits. Profiles and thresholds were
fixed in exploratory development, whose results motivated MG (\cref{sec:setup}).

\subsection{Learning source}\label{sec:learning}
The learning source runs on G30 (\cref{sec:setup}) and sets the controller's pre-start lead
and a floor $f_{t,a}$ under each air handler's static-pressure command. Its rules draw on optimal
start~\cite{seem2016return,kim2024optimalstart} and Guideline~36~\cite{ashrae2021g36}; rules and bounds were fixed in advance and never tuned on test data (\cref{app:learning}).

\paragraph{Learned optimal start.}
With the occupied comfort band $[T^{\mathrm{lo}}, T^{\mathrm{hi}}]$ and the air temperature $T_{z,t}$ of zone
$z \in \mathcal Z$, the morning deficit
\begin{equation}
  D_t = \frac{1}{|\mathcal Z|}\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z} \max\bigl(T^{\mathrm{lo}} - T_{z,t},\; 0,\; T_{z,t} - T^{\mathrm{hi}}\bigr)
  \label{eq:deficit}
\end{equation}
covers warm-up and cool-down alike. Under a first-order recovery $D(t+h) = D(t)\,e^{-h/\tau}$ with effective time constant
$\tau$, the lead that reaches a residual deficit $D^\star$ at the opening time $t^{\mathrm{o}}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_t = \tau \ln\frac{\max(D_t, D^\star)}{D^\star}.
  \label{eq:lead}
\end{equation}
On weekday mornings, within $t^{\mathrm{o}} - L^{\max} \le t < t^{\mathrm{o}} - L^{\min}$, conditioning starts at the first
step with $t^{\mathrm{o}} - t \le \lambda_t$; otherwise the controller keeps its own lead $L^{\min}$. The first hour of
each informative recovery updates a smoothed estimate of $\tau$ within $[\tau^{\min}, \tau^{\max}]$, and two consecutive mornings with a deficit above its
prediction by a margin return the start to the controller for the episode.

\paragraph{Pressure reset and precooling.}
During conditioning hours a zone warmer than $T^{\mathrm{hi}}$ with its cooling loop near saturation requests more air, and each
air-handling unit trims its floor $f_{t,a}$ without requests and raises it with them, within fixed bounds
(trim-and-respond). For a controller whose loss is mainly energy, a rule of the same form gives
learned night precooling: on warm nights with cool outdoor air, the layer lowers the unoccupied cooling limit within bounds
if the zone excess before opening, smoothed over earlier mornings without precooling, is positive (\cref{app:learning}).

\paragraph{A template for rules.}
Each mechanism is a bounded rule with five parts: the signal it reads, the condition that triggers it, the command it may
change, the bounds of that change, and the condition that returns control to the controller; \cref{tab:rules} also lists
what each needs from the controller. The start and precooling learn; the reset is bounded feedback. A new rule is written by hand for the loss term it targets, and \cref{sec:when} indicates
where it can pay: learned precooling was added this way when the start failed its development gate on Penguin,
and was then validated on unseen architectures.

\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Simulator and episodes.}
Experiments use the Smart Buildings Challenge simulator~\cite{goldfeder2024suite,goldfeder2026challenge} at
five-minute resolution with historical NOAA weather~\cite{smith2011isd}. An \emph{episode} pairs one generated building
with one weather window over a 30-day scoring horizon (8,640 steps, including unoccupied periods), and all policies of a
comparison run on the same episodes.

\paragraph{Controllers and comparators.}
The main controller, K07~\cite{malyshev2026k07}, is public and rule-based; we use two fixed configurations: the baseline
$B$, tuned during development for a first-workday objective, and G30, a stronger comparator selected from 64 candidates on
12 development episodes for the 30-day horizon. Profile schedules run $B$ for the first 19\,h, so their contrasts with fixed policies
include a start-up difference; the learning source runs on G30 from step zero and is also tested on other
controllers: Penguin~\cite{penguin2026deep}, which learns a zone model online and plans one step ahead; ZT
Best~\cite{malyshev2026zt}, the K07 author's later version (a maintenance test); Residual PPO~\cite{chilton2026resppo}, a rule-based
controller with a residual policy trained by reinforcement learning; and a graph network imitating it.

\paragraph{Evaluation sets.}
Each source was developed on its own sets and tested on sets frozen before they were run (\cref{tab:cohorts}). The profile
source was developed on cohorts of its library's six building families (\cref{app:cohorts}; chronology in
\cref{fig:timeline}); the mild-start pattern of the 2022 results motivated the gate, whose test on new 2021 episodes
was fixed in an internal protocol before any 2021 outcome was seen. The learning source was
developed on new architectures from the official scenario generator, checked once per mechanism on a held-out set,
and tested on a blind set first run after its rules and analyses were frozen; later tests and variants on its
scenes are marked as such (\cref{app:sets,app:moreresults}); ten blind architectures have composite floor plans and two out-of-distribution (OOD) shapes. Six
official training scenes serve for diagnosis; the Kaggle leaderboard scores hidden scenes.

\paragraph{Statistical analysis.}
Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from 3,000 cluster-bootstrap resamples of the stations of a cohort or the
architectures of an out-of-family set (\cref{app:inference}). The single pre-specified primary estimand of the profile source is
$\Delta_S(\mathrm{MG},\mathrm{TRIG2})$ on the 98 main 2021 episodes (\cref{eq:delta}), with success defined as a positive
estimate whose interval excludes zero; secondary contrasts are Holm-adjusted~\cite{holm1979}. Gains of the learning
source are pooled over the composite architectures (\cref{eq:gain}), with OOD reported separately; exact Wilcoxon tests check the ten blind ones (\cref{app:moreresults}). Each learning
mechanism had to pass a pre-specified development gate, and every pre-specified or registered test was frozen in a
protocol before its runs (\cref{tab:registry}).

On the 98 main 2021 episodes all five alternatives reduced loss relative to $B$ (\cref{tab:main}), the gate most, by 4.47\%

about as many profile changes as persistence, fewer than half those of daily reassignment, and had the lowest share of
episodes more than 1\% worse than $B$ among the adaptive schedules. The secondary \mbox{contrasts} agree; those against G30
and between daily and select-once have wide intervals and Holm-adjusted $p$-values

\paragraph{Re-selection paid mainly after mild starts.}\label{sec:regime}
On 2021 weather (\cref{fig:main}b), daily re-selection
beat select-once by 2.59\pp{} after mild starts (4.40\% versus 1.81\%) but was slightly worse after cold and hot starts,
and the gate, following its fixed mild-start rule, improved on $B$ in every class. This mild-start advantage had appeared in development, on 2023
weather, where daily re-selection was also slightly better after cold and hot starts (\cref{fig:main}a), and in both 2022 cohorts (\cref{tab:2022}). By branch, the gate gained by avoiding persistence-triggered switches after cold and
hot starts and by reacting faster after mild starts (\cref{app:inference}). The explanatory model of \cref{app:theory}
assumes longer spells after mild starts, which the 2021 weather does not support; post hoc, re-selection gained on cold or hot
days that left the mild profile and lost on mild days after cold or hot starts (\cref{tab:direction}).

\paragraph{Sensitivity checks held; replication inconclusive.}
The primary contrast survived re-weighting, subsets of building variants, another source of class labels and leaving out
one station or family (\cref{fig:contrasts}). The 20-episode extension was inconclusive ($-0.004$\pp, post hoc interval $[-0.45, +0.71]$); a registered replication
on 40 new episodes gave $+0.255$\pp{} ($[-0.037, +0.709]$), missing
its criterion of a lower bound above zero, so it stays inconclusive even though all 158 episodes, a registered secondary analysis, gave $+0.366$\pp{} ($[+0.206, +0.535]$;
\cref{app:inference,app:extended}).

improving 9 of the 10 composite and both OOD architectures; on the held-out set it gained 3.9\%, but only two of four

registered ($[0.10, 1.82]$, 7 of 10; exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.084$), and 0.95\% without the reset (8 of 10; Holm-adjusted $p = 0.023$ by bootstrap, $0.098$ by exact Wilcoxon); frozen at
1\,h, the start kept only a quarter of its gain. Its advantage built up over two weeks while the
pressure reset paid at once (\cref{fig:timetovalue}).

\paragraph{Other controllers also gained.}\label{sec:second}\label{sec:maintenance}
Penguin's~\cite{penguin2026deep} loss is 36\% lower than K07-G30's and 97\% energy and carbon; the K07 start
failed its development gate there, whereas learned night precooling, aimed at energy, reduced its loss by 1.88\% and
improved all 12 architectures. On ZT Best~\cite{malyshev2026zt}, the K07 author's later version, on the same blind scenes, the unchanged layer
gained 5.71\% ($[3.64, 7.55]$; all 12 improved), and its pre-specified maintenance rule held. On a trained neural
controller~\cite{chilton2026resppo}, itself better than K07-G30, it gained 3.37\%, 4.70 points less comfort loss
for 1.34 more in energy and carbon, and 3.55\% ($[1.82, 5.40]$) on a graph network imitating it, but the rule failed on both, with one or two architectures
more than 1\% worse. Alone, as registered, the pressure reset gained 2.99\% on the neural controller ($[1.60, 4.53]$, all 10
better) and the learned start 0.49\%, which caused both losses there (1.35, 1.15\%); the reset alone would have passed
the rule (\cref{app:moreresults}).

\subsection{When does the layer help?}\label{sec:when}
\paragraph{Largest gains on extreme days, losses on holidays.}\label{sec:erroranalysis}
Post hoc, the layer gained 4.1 and 4.2\% on the coldest and hottest tenth of days against 2.1\% otherwise, and slightly
more on Mondays than on other weekdays (3.1 against 2.8\%); six of its ten worst days were public holidays, unoccupied in the simulator but unknown to the controller, so its earlier start bought no comfort (\cref{fig:error}).

\paragraph{The gain followed the comfort share of the loss (post hoc).}\label{sec:depends}

Across the 36 blind episodes of G30, Spearman $\rho = 0.80$ ($[0.57, 0.89]$
by architecture; thirds in \cref{fig:when}a), and $\rho = 0.78$ across architectures, whereas their shared denominator
alone gives a mean $\rho$ of $-0.09$ when the loss reductions are permuted. By construction, the gain is the share times the part of the
targeted loss removed, 15--24\% on all three comfort controllers, minus the cost in the other terms
(\cref{eq:headroom}, \cref{tab:headroom}). The share thus bounds the
benefit, and the net gain unless the other terms improve; it measures the headroom rather than predicting the gain, though its first-week value ranked
the later gain ($\rho = 0.72$; exploratory, \cref{tab:earlyind}). On Penguin the
mean comfort share is 2.2\%, and learned precooling, aimed at energy and carbon, removed 1.82 points without costing
comfort. Comfort's 60\% weight matters: with equal weights the K07 layer would gain only 0.26\% (\cref{fig:when}b).

\paragraph{Five of seven registered predictions held.}
Before the ZT Best runs on the same scenes we registered three predictions: the gain rises with the comfort
share (held, $\rho = 0.93$), exceeds G30's gain as ZT Best's mean share does (held: 5.97\% against 2.69\% on all 36
episodes), and stays within $\pm 0.5\%$ in the lowest-share third (failed: 0.78\%). For the neural
controller three of four held (the lowest third: 0.70\%), and all four for the graph network imitating it, not an independent check (\cref{app:moreresults}).

\subsection{The leaderboard and an unoccupied setup}\label{sec:lb}

Tuning improved K07's Kaggle score by 2.6\%, to 0.01477; the learning source left it unchanged but made the neural controller's
1.0\% worse, against a registered prediction of no change (\cref{tab:lb}). A diagnosis of the official training scenes led to a Guideline~36 unoccupied cooling
setpoint that passed its development gate (\cref{tab:uns}) and scored 4.3\% below G30; on the blind set it lost 0.62\%,
and with the learned start gained 1.27\% over G30 but failed its pre-specified rule. As an untuned
third rule of the layer it added 0.38\%, with an interval spanning zero ($[-0.45, +1.49]$; \cref{app:moreresults}).

%======================================================================
\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================
\paragraph{Which source, when.}
Neither source wins by default (\cref{tab:when}). Profiles paid only on buildings like those they were tuned
on; on new buildings a learning rule paid where the controller left loss in the term the rule targets and
added little elsewhere. This gives a check that needs no new experiment: before attaching a rule, measure the
share of the controller's loss in the term it targets. Unless the other terms improve, the rule cannot gain
more than this share, and our comfort rules removed 15--24\% of the targeted loss (\cref{sec:when}); on the
official training scenes, where K07 and the neural controller leave 0.2--4.6\% and 2.5\% of their loss in
comfort, this leaves about 1\% or less, which fits their flat or negative leaderboard results, though the
hidden scenes' losses are unknown. The account is post hoc and only partly confirmed by registered
predictions, and gains chosen on a leaderboard need not transfer, as the unoccupied setup showed.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The two sources were tested on different sets, not head-to-head on matched episodes, and were not combined. The evidence is limited (\cref{tab:evidence}): the blind intervals rest on 10 composite
architectures from one generator; the layer's pre-specified rule failed on the held-out set, the neural controller and its
imitation (\cref{sec:learn}); the account of \cref{sec:when} was tested across controllers on the same scenes
rather than on new buildings; the gate was formulated after inspecting
earlier results and frozen internally, its extension and its registered replication were inconclusive, its intervals
ignore the shared building families, and loss components were not archived for its later cohorts. The layer's value also depends on the objective weights (\cref{sec:when}); it never ran on an
untuned controller, and G30 came from a budget-limited search. Longer leads in deep cold triggered the safety freeze in
slow buildings; the start rule acts on the mean zone, which can hide the cost in a few zones, and its time constant also
absorbs outdoor temperature and season (\cref{app:moreresults}).

\paragraph{Future work.}
Decomposing the controller's loss by term and hour and attaching the mechanism aimed at the largest term, as we did by
hand for Penguin and the unoccupied setup, would systematise transfer; a start rule weighing its night-time cost
against the expected comfort deficit would limit the losses above, and stopping it would need a runtime proxy of its
gain. Decisions per zone, a holiday calendar and real buildings come next, where bounds and freezes are no safety guarantee.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================
We studied two sources of values for a bounded supervisory layer over an unmodified rule-based HVAC
controller: re-selection from a weather-conditioned profile library and online estimation with feedback.
Within the library's building families, a one-time gate that re-selects daily only after mild starts modestly
beat persistence in a pre-specified test, but its registered replication was inconclusive, and outside those
families the frozen profiles did not transfer. On unseen architectures, a learned start with a pressure reset,
a few bounded rules with no offline training, reduced the loss of a tuned fixed controller by 2.82\%;
unchanged, the layer also reduced pooled loss on a later version of the controller and on a trained neural
controller, where its per-architecture criterion failed, and an energy-targeted rule helped a model-based
controller. The gains are modest and not universal: on the competition leaderboard the comfort rules gave no
gain or a loss. An exact decomposition bounds a rule's benefit by the share of the controller's loss in the
term it targets; post hoc, gains followed this share, and registered predictions on shared scenes partly
confirmed it. This points to selective, controller-aware supervision: measuring where a controller loses
before attaching a rule. The study gives no rule for choosing between the two sources and no safety guarantee,
and all results come from simulation. Our code, episode definitions and per-episode results are available at
\mbox{\url{https://github.com/AsteriosPanoras/supervisory-layer-hvac}}.
